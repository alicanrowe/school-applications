# Recommendation letters

## Plan
1. List every program's letter count, who it wants (faculty, supervisor, employer, client), how letters are collected (portal invite, email, mail, scholarship form) and the due date.
2. The number of distinct writers needed = the largest count any single program asks for; reuse writers across programs where their perspective fits.
3. Map writers to programs: research labs want someone who saw you do research; MFAs want people who know your studio work; craft schools want instructors or employers who can speak to work ethic at the bench. No family.
4. Ask at least 3 to 4 weeks before the first letter deadline, more over holidays. One ask can cover several schools.
5. Portal invites: the applicant enters each writer in each school's portal, which emails the writer a link. Enter writers early; letters can't start until the invite goes out.
6. Give each writer: the list of programs with deadlines, CV, portfolio link, a few lines on what each program values, and a reminder of specific work they saw.
7. Track each letter per school as invited, received. A polite nudge from the applicant a week before each deadline; a thank-you after.

## Request note template
> Hi [Name],
>
> I'm applying to [programs] for [term] and would be grateful if you'd write me a letter of recommendation. You know [the work they saw], and I think you can speak to [the quality that program values].
>
> The programs and dates are: [program, date; program, date]. Each school will email you its own upload link, and one letter can be reused with small edits. I've attached my CV, portfolio and a short note on what each program looks for.
>
> If the timing doesn't work, I completely understand. Thank you either way.
>
> [Name]
