# Tailoring the portfolio and essays

Same person, different reader. Decide what each reader is judging, then lead with the evidence for that.

## What each kind of program judges
| Kind | Judges | Portfolio leads with | Essay leads with |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Research lab master's | Can you pose a question and build toward it? Do you fit a named group? | Systems and prototypes with the question each answers; tools others can use; anything published or open-sourced | The research question, then why these specific groups and faculty |
| Studio MFA | A body of resolved work and a point of view you can sustain through two years of critique | Finished physical pieces photographed well; a little process; few renders | The work you want to make and why it needs this studio |
| Craft and trade school | Commitment, work ethic, hand skill, readiness for the bench | Work made with hand and machine tools, joinery close-ups, in-process shots | Concrete experience (tools, joints, pieces) and the career it leads to |

## Portfolio steps
1. Inventory every piece: title, year, medium, whether it is built and photographed, in progress, or design/render only, and the best image.
2. For each program, filter by its rules (count, format, platform, video limits), then rank by what that program judges.
3. Sequence: strongest first, end strong; group a project's images together; one idea per image.
4. Captions: title, year, material, size; one line of context at most for MFAs and craft schools. Research portfolios get a short paragraph per project: question, what you built, what you learned.
5. Fill gaps early: book a photo session of finished pieces (plain background, even light) and shoot a short making clip if a video is allowed.

## Essay steps
1. Write down the one through line of the applicant's work in a sentence; every essay is that line seen from the school's side.
2. Outline each essay to its own prompt, in the order the prompt asks, sized to its limit (about 250 to 275 words for one page double spaced).
3. Use specifics from the work: names of pieces, materials, mentors, numbers.
4. Check each school's AI policy before drafting prose. Where AI-written content is banned, give outlines, questions and line edits only.
5. Read each draft against the prompt and the limit before calling it done.
